Why replace batting

Batting drains. It does not recover.

Commodity outdoor cushions have long been built on layered densified polyester batting. That construction is inexpensive and moisture-tolerant. It is also the reason a dining chair looks tired by mid-season.

Under repeated compression — dining, deep seating, chaise, marine cockpit — batting packs down, loses loft, and presents a progressively flatter sit. Edges go soft. Moisture lingers in the fiber slab. Guests feel it before they name it.

We specify high-recovery cellular cores to displace that construction: open-cell reticulated Cloud Dry for wet exposure, high-resiliency polyurethane where a plush recovering sit is required, and CNC-bonded composite sandwiches when the brief needs both.

  • Collapse Center loft drops after weeks of load cycles. The silhouette reads deflated on walk-through.
  • Flatten Boxed edges round off. Welt lines wave. The cushion stops looking like furniture and starts looking like a pad.
  • Moisture retention Fiber slabs hold a “wet sandwich” hand after rain. Drainage is qualitative; there is no published CFM rate for commodity batting.
  • Short outdoor service life Recovery and fatigue resistance trail modern cellular polyurethane systems. The cover is blamed; the core is the problem.

Invisible luxury

Aesthetics that hold after rain

Beauty in outdoor cushioning is the visible consequence of a correctly specified core, cut, and cover. Cloud Dry is the luxury the guest never names — a recovering crown, square boxing, and a welt that still tracks true.

Waterfall fronts
CNC-rolled seat-to-bullnose curves that read as furniture, not slab-cut foam wrapped in fabric.
0.5" center crown
Soft visual loft at mid-seat that also sheds standing water under covers and after storms.
Precision edges
±0.125" boxed perimeter discipline. Corners hold radius instead of rounding into batting mush.
Hinge routes
Fold geometry that keeps a chaise silhouette unbroken when reclined or stacked — no jagged kerfs telegraphing through the cover.
